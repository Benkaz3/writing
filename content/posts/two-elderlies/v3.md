How often do you see two elderly men walking inside an apartment complex yard holding hands? Not very often I guess, even in 2026 Vietnam where people are a lot more open about sexual orientation. I don’t know if they are a couple or just very close friends. The thing about our culture is that older generations don’t have that clear distinction. People can be very passionate, physically about how they interact with one another. The fear of touching another person of the same sex is really not there, at least for the older generation. They were born and raised in a time where awareness of homosexuality is almost non-existent. So seeing same sex people holding hands in public is only the signal of their strong bond. Then the real question one must ask is “is ignorance a bliss?”, in other words, “does knowing less help create less social issues - specifically discrimination?”

Let’s put it this way, would you enjoy a TV show as much if you work in the who industry? The answer is probably no. Simply because you know the things that most people don’t. One example is a line that’s seemingly said at the spur of the moment but it may have been scripted. Another example could be an hilarious informercial part that was revised over and over again by the brand people. Knowing how things work very often makes you enjoy the final products less - even dislike them. 

Is this always true in everything around us? Let’s circle back to the discrimination  and answer the question “If one does not know about homosexuality, would they not discriminate?”. The elderlies consider their holding hand a normal bonding gesture because of their ignorance. But how would they react to the concept of a romantic relationship between two same sex people? My guess is that they would freak out and no longer view it as “normal”. Our instinct always puts us in alert mode when we encounter unfamiliar things. And often, we consider it a threat. Then ignorance is no longer a bliss because as soon as we learn about the true nature of the subject, the issue of discrimination is born. 

So what is the difference between this and the TV show case? Does involvement in the making of something make that something less enjoyable? Personally when I cook a dish, I enjoy the dish. But do I enjoy it more when someone else cooks it? The answer depends on how delicious the dish is to my taste. In this case, making the dish is an independent variable. It does not have an impact on how much the dish is enjoyed. 

Then we must break down the case of the TV show because it may have more components. Let’s simplify it. A writer who works on her script multiple times to perfect it, would she be happier with the final version? In most cases, the final script would have been improved significantly. So what are the other components that would make her enjoy the show less? Two main things - one is the commercial influence on the final product, modifying her script, the other is the nature of TV show creation in which deceiving the audience could a a part of what makes it attractive. 

The TV show is a business. It needs to make money to pay the people who make it. It’s not a pure form of creativity like a painting which may not pay the artist until after he has passed away. A TV show needs to make money as it’s aired. In order for that to happen, it usually requires commercial partnership with brands. And brands have a say in how the show is made. This creates a co-creation relationship with the show makers, the writer in our case. The final product is no longer her own but commercially modified. This often negatively impacts the creative quality, at least in the eyes of the writer. 

The other factor that also makes the show less enjoyable is the unsurprised elements introduced to the show. The difference is the elements often are created by the writer herself, making the surprise moments or the twists meaningless. Many movies open with “based on real life events” to set up the mindset for the audience. If these are historical events then the story is so much closer to the audience - sometimes, relatable. This type of storytelling is effective, requiring the audience to proactively think of the events and expand their validation and thinking process. So yes, the writer knows these are fictional masked as true events to create creatively better product. 

This means the two cases are very different and do not argue if “ignorance is bliss” from equal perspectives. That does not mean the saying is wrong in all cases, but at least it is in the context of this analysis.
