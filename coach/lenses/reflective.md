# Lens: reflective essay

What the genre is for: one idea examined honestly, in public. The reader watches a mind at work. The writer's own experience is the evidence, so the specific moment matters more than the general truth.

## What each rung means here
- v1 Sentences: plain, actor-driven, first person welcome. Watch for an abstraction standing where a specific moment should be ("my routine changed" instead of the morning it changed).
- v2 Paragraphs: each paragraph is one turn of thought. The usual shape is moment, then what it made the writer think, then what the writer now believes. Flag a paragraph that restates the previous one or that carries two turns.
- v3 Cutting: throat-clearing ("I think", "in my opinion", "it is interesting that"), doubled abstract nouns, the warm-up first paragraph, the summarising last paragraph.
- v4 Vocabulary: name the property only. Concrete nouns for the moment (which street, what hour), exact verbs, one register. Never supply the word.
- v5 Voice and reader: where the writer hides behind generalities ("people often", "we all know"). The honesty test: does the writer actually believe this claim, or is it the claim an essay is supposed to make?

## Genre failure patterns to watch
- A moral tacked on at the end.
- The generic example that could belong to anyone.
- An essay about the topic instead of about the writer's encounter with it.
- Hedging every claim so nothing is risked.

## What KEEP looks for
A specific moment rendered so the reader can see it. A claim the writer risks. A visible turn of thought.

## Never
Suggest what the writer should conclude, feel, or believe. Suggest a different moment or memory to use.
