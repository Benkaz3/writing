# Lens: analytical

What the genre is for: a reader can test the claim. The reasoning is on the page, in order, with the evidence it rests on.

## What each rung means here
- v1 Sentences: actor plus verb, no chains of nominalisations ("the implementation of the evaluation of..."). A claim sentence states the claim, not the topic ("this essay discusses leadership" is a topic).
- v2 Paragraphs: claim, evidence, explanation, significance. The topic sentence is a claim a reader could disagree with. One paragraph, one claim. Transitions show logic ("because", "but", "which means"), not sequence ("next", "another point").
- v3 Cutting: "it is important to note", "in today's society", the thesis restated as the conclusion, evidence quoted at twice the length it needs.
- v4 Vocabulary: name the property only. Precise terms used consistently, intensifiers removed ("very", "clearly", "extremely"), hedges sized to the evidence. Never supply the word.
- v5 Voice and reader: formal register, no contractions. The sceptical reader: at each claim, what would a reader who disagrees say, and is it answered? A counterargument conceded and then met.

## Quotation and citation integrity
Verify every quotation word for word against the source before anything else. A misquotation, or a paraphrase inside quotation marks, is always flag 1. Citation form must be consistent within the piece.

## Genre failure patterns to watch
- Evidence dropped in without explanation of what it shows.
- Claims that are really topics.
- An author's credentials standing in for a judgement of their logic.
- The conclusion that only repeats the thesis.

## What KEEP looks for
A claim a reader could test. A counterargument conceded and then answered. A piece of evidence explained rather than displayed.

## Coursework reminder
If the piece is a course assignment, the course's own rules on AI assistance and logging apply on top of this file. Say so once, in one line, on the first pass of such a piece.

## Never
Supply a claim, a counterargument, a source, a piece of evidence, or a judgement on whether the thesis is right.
