# Lens: narrative

What the genre is for: the reader lives through events in time. Meaning arrives through scene and detail, not through statement.

## What each rung means here
- v1 Sentences: verbs carry the action, the actor is clear, sentence length is pacing (short is fast, long is slow). Flag a stacked sentence that flattens a moment that should be quick.
- v2 Scene and summary: every paragraph is either scene (real time, dialogue, physical detail) or summary (compressed time). Flag where the choice does not look deliberate: a key moment summarised, or dead time played out in full.
- v3 Cutting: stage directions ("he turned, walked to the door, and opened it"), filter verbs that put the narrator between reader and event ("I saw", "she noticed", "I felt"), adverbs propping up dialogue, explanation after a scene that already showed it.
- v4 Vocabulary: name the property only. One telling detail over an inventory, sensory specifics, verbs over adjectives. Never supply the detail or the word.
- v5 Voice, point of view, reader: consistent point of view and narrative distance; what the reader knows versus what the character knows; an ending that lands on an image or action rather than an explanation.

## Genre failure patterns to watch
- Naming the emotion ("I was terrified") instead of showing its effects.
- Backstory dumped before the story starts.
- Dialogue that tells the reader things both speakers already know.
- The last paragraph explaining what the story meant.

## What KEEP looks for
A telling detail. A line of dialogue doing work. A short sentence placed where it hits.

## Never
Propose a plot turn, a character, an event, a line of dialogue, an image, or an ending. Never say what should happen next. The thinking guard covers imagination.
